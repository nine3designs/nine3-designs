Why your rebrand isn't working (yet)

15 August 2026 · 4 min read · by Natalie

A new logo goes live, everyone says lovely things about it and nothing changes. No more enquiries, no different conversations, same old numbers. Before you blame the design, check which of these three things actually happened.

1. Only the logo changed

The logo shipped, but the business cards, social templates, signage, email signature and website still speak the old language. A rebrand that lives in one file on your desktop is not a rebrand ...it is a preview. Map your touchpoints, rank them by how often customers meet them, and roll the new identity out in that order.

2. The strategy was skipped

If the brief was “make it look more modern” rather than “appeal to X, justify Y, move away from Z”, then nothing was really being fixed. New colours on top of old confusion is decoration. Write the positioning in one sentence first: who it is for, what you promise, why you and not the alternative. Then design against it.

3. The experience did not catch up

Your brand is a promise; your customer experience decides whether people believe it. If the identity now whispers “considered and premium” but enquiries sit unanswered for three days, people believe the three days. A rebrand is the start of a behaviour change, not a substitute for one.

So what now?

None of these mean the design was wrong. They mean the rebrand is unfinished which is genuinely good news, because finishing is cheaper than starting again. Audit the touchpoints, pin the strategy sentence above your desk, and give the aligned version ninety days of consistency before you judge it.

If you want an honest outside eye on which of the three is yours, that is a conversation I am always happy to have.
