How long does it take to build a website?

19 October 2026 · 5 min read · by Natalie

The honest answer: two to six weeks for most small business websites, and the designer is rarely the bottleneck. The part that stretches every timeline is the same every time — waiting on content, photos and decisions. So here is where the weeks actually go, and how to keep them from multiplying.

Where the time goes

  • Discovery & quote (1–3 days). We talk about what the site needs to do, I price it fixed, you say go.
  • Content gathering (the big one). Words, photos, reviews, menus, price lists — whatever exists. If it does not exist yet, this is where projects breathe.
  • Design (3–7 days). Homepage first, so we lock the look before building anything else.
  • Build & pages (1–2 weeks). Turning the approved design into every page, on desktop and mobile.
  • Testing & launch (2–3 days). Forms, buttons, links, browsers, then it goes live.
Websites rarely run late because of design. They run late because "I will send the photos this week" took three weeks.

What makes a project fast

After enough launches I can tell you exactly which clients go live quickest. They are not the ones who rush me — they are the ones who:

  1. Decide one decision-maker. Two opinions double every round of feedback; five turn weeks into months.
  2. Send everything in one go. A folder with photos, the logo and one honest page of words beats ten "quick questions" emails.
  3. Give feedback in one pass. Collect the team's comments, send one list. Round three of piecemeal tweaks is where timelines quietly die.
  4. Know their next step. "Get a quote" or "book a call" — deciding the goal up front removes a hundred small debates later.

A realistic promise

For a typical small business site with content ready, I aim for two to three weeks from go-ahead to launch. With a booking system or online payments, add a week or two. If anyone promises a week, ask what they will skip — it is usually the testing, the mobile checks or the copy, and it shows.

Got a date in mind — an opening day, a wedding fair, a season start? Tell me the deadline and I will tell you honestly whether it fits.
