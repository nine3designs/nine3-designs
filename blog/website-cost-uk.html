How Much Does a Website Cost in the UK? (2026 Guide for Small Businesses)

Nine3 Designs · Updated September 2026 · 8 minute read

The short answer£1,200–£8,000 for a professional small business website in the UK — with most regional studios landing around £2,000–£6,000. DIY builders start from about £10/month, but as you'll see, cheap and good are rarely the same thing.

That's a wide range, we know. So in this guide we'll break down exactly what you get at each price point, the six things that move the number up or down, and the hidden costs that catch business owners out every year.

No jargon, no scare tactics — just honest numbers from a studio that builds these sites every week.

A quick word on honesty: anyone quoting a single magic number without asking about your business is guessing. Here's what actually moves the price.

The 6 things that change your website cost

1. Template or 100% custom design

A template site changes the colours on someone else's design. A custom site is designed around your brand, your customers and your goals from a blank page. Custom costs more — but it's the difference between renting a flat and building your own home. One of them looks like every other business on your street.

2. Size and features

A 5-page site (Home, Services, About, Work, Contact) costs far less than a 20-page site with bookings, member areas or an online shop. E-commerce adds the most: payments, security and product management all take extra time.

3. Who writes the words?

Copywriting is the most-forgotten cost in web design. Writing it yourself is free-ish (but plan a full weekend); professional copy runs £200–£800. Good copy converts visitors into enquiries — a smart place to spend, not save.

4. Branding, photos and content

Got a logo, colours and decent photos already? That keeps costs down. Need them created? Budget for it — a branding-plus-website package usually beats buying each separately.

5. SEO foundations

A website Google can't find is a leaflet in a drawer. Keyword structure, metadata, fast loading and a Google Business Profile should be built in from day one — not bolted on later for a premium.

6. Ongoing care

Domains (~£10/year), hosting (~£60–£200/year) and occasional updates. Always ask what happens after launch — a website is a living thing, not a one-off purchase.

"But I saw websites advertised for £200…"

Here's what bargain-basement usually means: a template used hundreds of times, slow loading, no support when it breaks at 9pm on a bank holiday, and no strategy behind a single pixel. A website isn't a digital brochure — it's your hardest-working salesperson.

The math that matters: if your site brings in just two extra customers a month, a £2,000 website can pay for itself within weeks. Cheap sites rarely bring in anyone — which makes them the most expensive websites of all.

How to budget: the 5-point checklist

  • Decide your must-haves — pages, features, who writes the copy
  • Get three quotes — and compare what's included, not just the price
  • Ask who owns everything — domain, hosting, files (you should, always)
  • Ask about aftercare — updates, backups, support, response times
  • Budget £100–£300/year for running costs — "free forever" usually means you're the product

Frequently asked questions

How much should a small business spend on a website?

Most established UK small businesses spend £1,500–£4,000 on a professional website. New businesses can start smaller and upgrade as they grow — just make sure whatever you launch can be expanded later.

Is a cheap website worth it?

Rarely. Cheap websites usually mean templates, slow loading and no support — and they tend to cost more in lost customers than they save. "Affordable" should mean good value, not low quality.

Do I own my website?

You should own your domain name and your content, always. Some DIY builders keep control in their platform, which makes leaving awkward. Ask this before you sign anything.

How much does website maintenance cost in the UK?

Expect £10–£50/month for basic care (updates, backups, small tweaks) or £60–£200/year for essentials like hosting and domain renewals if you maintain it yourself.

Can I build a website for free?

Almost. Free builders exist but add branding you can't remove, limit features and lock you in. Fine for testing an idea — not great for a business that wants to be taken seriously.
