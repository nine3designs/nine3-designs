Logo Design Cost UK: What Should You Actually Pay in 2026?

November 2026 · 8 min read · by Natalie

Ask five people what a logo costs and you’ll get five wildly different answers. A logo-making app says £30. A freelancer says £300. An agency quotes £5,000 and talks about “discovery workshops” for a week first. All three of them are looking at the same job. So what exactly are you paying for?

We’re a small design studio in Rotherham, so yes, we sell logos, and you should read this with that in mind. We’ve still done our best to make it genuinely useful, including the moments when a cheap logo is absolutely the right call.

What a logo actually costs in the UK in 2026

Here’s the honest landscape, from free to eye-watering:

Who you hire Typical price What you actually get
DIY logo maker or template site £0–£50 A template tweaked by software: the same starting point as thousands of other businesses
Marketplace freelancer £50–£300 Semi-custom concepts, often limited revisions and unclear ownership terms
Experienced freelancer £300–£800 Original work, a handful of concepts, decent file sets
Small design studio £249–£1,195 Strategy-led, 100% custom work, full file set, and someone who answers the phone
Branding agency £2,500–£10,000+ Full discovery, research, brand guidelines and rollout management

Why the gap is so wide

Strategy and research

The expensive end isn’t charging more for the same drawing. Before a single sketch, a proper process looks at who your customers are, what your competitors look like, and what your logo has to do in the real world: sit on a van, shrink to a favicon, work in one colour on an invoice. Skip that research and the design is decoration; do it and the design has a job description.

Originality

Template logos are cheap because the drawing already exists: you’re licensing it, slightly recoloured. Original work starts from a blank page with your business in it. That matters more than it sounds: templates cluster, and there’s a real chance another business in your town looks dishearteningly similar.

The file set

A logo isn’t one file, it’s a toolkit: vector versions that scale to the side of a van, a simplified mark for social avatars, a one-colour version for embroidery, a favicon for the browser tab. Cheap providers often hand over a single low-resolution image, and you’ll pay someone else to rebuild it the first time a printer asks for “the vector”.

Revisions and rights

Check what’s included. How many rounds of changes? Who owns the final artwork? A surprising number of cheap deals leave you with a licence rather than ownership, which gets awkward if you ever want to trademark it or take it to another designer.

What a cheap logo quietly costs you

None of this means expensive is automatically better. It means cheap has failure modes worth knowing about. Most businesses replace their logo two or three times, and each redo costs the price of doing it properly the first time, plus the small identity confusion of customers seeing a new mark every couple of years. Template collisions mean you can look identical to a competitor. And a logo that turns to mush at small sizes quietly sabotages every profile picture and invoice header it touches.

If you’re pre-revenue and testing an idea, use a free logo maker with our blessing. Plan to replace it when the idea proves itself. That’s what it’s for.

How to choose, whatever your budget

  • Ask what research happens before the design (there should be some).
  • Insist on full vector files, in writing, as part of the price.
  • Check you’ll own the final artwork, not just license it.
  • Ask to see the logo at favicon size before you approve it.
  • Treat “unlimited revisions” as a question, not a bonus: it often means nobody’s steering.

The bottom line

In 2026, £249–£449 buys a small UK studio’s full attention: custom work, a proper file set and a human who knows your business. Below that, you’re renting a template. Above that, you’re buying process most small businesses don’t need yet. Pay for the thinking, check the files, own the artwork. The price tag matters far less than what sits behind it.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Is a £249 logo actually custom?

Yes: custom means drawn for your business after a proper brief. What you’re not paying for at small-studio prices is agency overhead: offices, account managers and research decks. The drawing itself is still original, and the strategy is still a conversation with the person holding the pen.

Do I own my logo afterwards?

With us, yes: full copyright transfers to you on final payment, in writing. If you’re comparing quotes elsewhere, ask this exact question: “do I own the artwork or license it?” The answer changes what a cheap logo is really worth.

Is a logo the same as a brand?

No. The logo is the signature, the brand is everything it signs: colours, fonts, tone of voice, how your socials and invoices look. If budget is tight, start with a strong logo and grow the system around it. Our Branding Starter package exists precisely for that path.

Why do agencies charge £5,000+?

Mostly process weight: weeks of research, positioning work, stakeholder workshops and full brand guidelines, for organisations where the brand decision has to survive a boardroom. It’s the right buy for some businesses and overkill for most small ones. An honest studio will tell you which you are.
