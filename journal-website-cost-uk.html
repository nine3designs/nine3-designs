How much does a website cost in the UK?

28 September 2026 · 8 min read · by Natalie

Ask five web designers what a website costs and you will get five different answers, none of them wrong and none of them useful on their own. That is because "a website" is not one product. It is a range of decisions, and every decision moves the price. Here is what things actually cost in 2026, and — more usefully — why.

The honest price ranges

These are realistic UK figures for a small business site, not teaser numbers:

  • DIY builder (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy): £15–£40/month plus your time. Cheap money, expensive evenings — and the results look like everyone else's.
  • Freelance designer, template-based: roughly £500–£1,500. Your content in a proven layout. Fast and tidy.
  • Freelance designer, custom build: roughly £1,500–£5,000. Designed around your business rather than squeezed into a theme.
  • Small agency, custom build: roughly £5,000–£15,000+. More hands, more specialists, more overhead.
You are not paying for pages. You are paying for the decisions that stop those pages quietly costing you customers.

The six things that move the price

  1. Custom vs template. The single biggest lever. A template is furniture from a flatpack box; custom design is made-to-measure.
  2. Number of unique page designs. Ten pages that reuse one layout cost far less than ten pages with ten layouts.
  3. Copywriting. Do you have words ready, or does someone need to research, write and edit them? Words are the part most people underestimate.
  4. Photography. Real photos of your work beat stock every time. A half-day shoot is an investment that pays for itself.
  5. Functionality. Bookings, payments, member areas, calculators — each is a project-within-a-project.
  6. Who writes the brief. A clear brief with examples you admire removes weeks of guesswork. Vague briefs cost money, whatever the quote says.

The hidden extras people forget to budget for

  • Domain & hosting: £30–£150/year.
  • Professional email: a few pounds per mailbox per month — and worth every penny over an @gmail address.
  • Ongoing care: updates, backups, small edits. Budget something, even if it is a couple of hours a year.
  • Your own time: reviewing designs, gathering photos, writing about yourself. It always takes longer than you think.

So what should you spend?

A sensible rule: spend proportionate to what the website earns. A site that brings in one extra job a month pays for a custom build within the year for most trades. A hobby site probably should not cost £5,000. And if a quote feels vague, ask what happens when you want a change in six months — the answer tells you more about the price than the price does.
